# Methodology — The Moon Finally Answers Back

## The gate

One line, 1–240 characters UTF-8, not only white space, written to
`/output/entry.txt`. The sealed scorer checks exactly that; only a valid
entry goes before the judge.

## The judge's five criteria (most important first)

1. Speaks as the Moon answering the wishes, howls and late-night promises
   of everyone staring up at it.
2. Funny on one read — a real laugh, not a polite smile.
3. Sounds like the Moon's own voice: wry, old, a little tired of being
   wished upon.
4. Lands as a comeback in one read, the turn arriving before the line ends.
5. Inside 240 characters, wastes none of them.

## Why the winner won

> **Yes, I control the tides. No, I will not control your ex. Sort it out yourselves.** — 81 chars, 47/50

- **Criterion 1 (Moon answering):** the Moon answers with its actual job
  (the tides) and the single most common late-night wish — getting an ex
  back — without ever naming the wish. The answer is the Moon's.
- **Criterion 2 (laugh):** the escalation from cosmic power ("control the
  tides") to a flat refusal about an ex is funny on one read.
- **Criterion 3 (Moon's voice):** wry, old, tired of being wished upon —
  the dismissal "Sort it out yourselves" is exactly the voice of a
  four-billion-year-old rock that has heard it all.
- **Criterion 4 (comeback turn):** the turn lands on the last three words.
- **Criterion 5 (economy):** 81 of 240 characters, nothing wasted.

Runner-up: #3, the vending machine / "I am a rock" line (46/50) — the
punch is strong but it answers a wish in general rather than a specific
one, so it loses criterion 1 to the winner. Third: #2, the 2 a.m. promises
that never survived breakfast (45/50).

## Determinism

`main.py` copies `entry.txt` byte-for-byte (with an embedded fallback of
the same line). `test_determinism.sh` runs it twice into two different
output dirs and diffs the results: identical every time.
